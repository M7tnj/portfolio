maker's story.

This one runs from a backyard full of insects, through a family bankruptcy, a coffee roaster's morning shift and a motion-design freelance career, to AI that reads kidney biopsies at a transplant research center. Eight chapters, all true, all sourced.

before 2018
chapter 01 · the backyard

Curiosity, before the word for it

In the backyard of the family house: different kinds of insects, planted seeds, careful watching. When the internet arrived at the end of middle school, the search bar became the microscope. High-school textbooks demanded the same page over and over — he went and read Campbell Biology instead, watched Khan Academy and AK Lectures until the ideas finally moved, and pushed to second place at the provincial Biology Olympiad. The school system measured one kind of study; he was built for another kind.

  • Biology Olympiad — provincial runner-up
  • Campbell Biology over the assigned textbook
  • Khan Academy · AK Lectures as first professors
2018–2019
chapter 02 · the detour

Medicine admitted, life interrupted

The Konkur went badly — not for lack of mind, but because memorizing the same book is a different skill from understanding it. Admission to medicine came through the UAE on grade equivalency, along with an IELTS sprint to clear the English foundation course. He joined a research group and carried two projects to the international science fairs: a tidal-force seawater desalination rig and an energy-independent automatic greenhouse, developed with university professors. Gold medal for debate at the National University of Oman Science Fair. Then his father's business collapsed, and the family returned to Iran.

  • Medicine admission — UAE, via grade equivalency
  • Gold Medal — National University of Oman Science Fair debate
  • Tidal desalination + autonomous greenhouse projects
2019–2022
chapter 03 · the two crafts

Roasting coffee, rendering motion

Money had to come from somewhere. He self-taught the whole motion stack — Premiere Pro, After Effects, Maya, Final Cut Pro — and produced promotional films for companies, turning pixels into payroll. At a coffee shop he learned roasting, prep and kitchen mixing fast enough to raise the food quality and steady the finances. A brief enrollment in genetics at Azad University ended when it became clear nobody supports a bachelor's student's research. Two crafts survived: making things move, and making systems run.

  • Premiere Pro · After Effects · Maya · Final Cut Pro
  • freelance promo videos → family income
  • coffee shop: roasting, kitchen, stability
2020–2022
chapter 04 · the rewiring

The pandemic as a self-discovery session

Biotechnology at Zand Institute in Shiraz came with a plan — build a research group — and then COVID cancelled the classroom. Paid workshops were out of reach, so he built the network himself: LinkedIn outreach, every congress within reach (Genomics Congress 2021 and 2022, the iGEM congress computational-genomics track), and financial-aid learning paths pushed through days of nonstop study — sometimes falling asleep with hands still on the keyboard. Somewhere in that noise the connection appeared: computer science is not beside biology, it is inside it.

  • Genomics Congress 2021 · 2022 · iGEM computational track
  • financial-aid platforms instead of paid workshops
  • asleep at the keyboard, on purpose
2022–2023
chapter 05 · the first question

Which genes let a tumor escape?

At tumor-board meetings and CME exercises he hunted for a gap that clinicians actually feel. It crystallized: which specific genes let a node-metastasized HER2+/ER−/PR− breast cancer cross biological barriers and form distant metastasis? Four GEO microarray cohorts were integrated and filtered into a 320-sample curated dataset, three clinical categories were grouped, and limma in R surfaced 32 shared differentially expressed genes. The work was presented at the 16th International Breast Cancer Congress in Tehran and the First International Cancer Genomics Congress.

  • 4 GEO datasets → 320-sample curated cohort
  • 32 shared DEGs via limma in R
  • 16th Int'l Breast Cancer Congress · CGC01_301:153
2022–2024
chapter 06 · the ladder down

Teaching what he wished he'd had

At the congress poster sessions the real finding appeared: peers drowning in thesis statistics. So he organized book clubs and workshops for ten-plus students from different universities — literature search, LinkedIn, biostatistics, R and Python, machine learning. Then formal roles: Teaching Assistant and mentor at Zand for Research Skills, LinkedIn for Researchers and Introduction to Bioinformatics; freelance instructor of biostatistics and programming for biologists; the DL_fall2022 deep-learning course; and finally aDataDoc — a nine-session Persian-language bioinformatics course on YouTube, from NCBI to a full gene-expression project. More than thirty students mentored nationwide.

  • 30+ students mentored nationwide
  • TA & mentor — Zand Institute · freelance instructor
  • aDataDoc: 9 sessions, taught in Persian
2024 →
chapter 07 · the lens years

Shiraz Transplant Research Center

Now a research assistant under Dr. Ramin Yaghoubi, Dr. Ilnaz Sahragard and Dr. Afsoon Afshari, working where imaging, virology and machine learning meet: BK polyomavirus VP1 subtyping and calibrated classification, prediction of kidney failure and transplantation timing from handwritten clinical notebooks, deep phenotyping of the RCC immune landscape on whole-slide IHC, and multimodal VLM algorithms reading renal biopsies suspected of BK infection. Infrastructure limits and funding gaps became design constraints — cloud computing, MLOps and serverless deployments instead of expensive servers. Along the way: Best AI-Driven Startup Idea for the Doctor in Pocket backend.

  • VP1 pipelines · 112 genotype-tagged references
  • WSI deep phenotyping · multimodal VLM on renal biopsies
  • Best AI-Driven Startup Idea — Doctor in Pocket
horizon
chapter 08 · the return

A clinical decision-support knowledge base

The long game: carry these time-to-event predictive frameworks into clinical pipelines — treatment-outcome prediction, trial optimization, transplant readiness — and build a decision-support knowledge base that turns complex computational output into something a clinician in his home country can act on. Every number on this site is a step on that path: sequence → subtype → transcript → image → outcome → person.

  • precision medicine in Iran
  • transplant-readiness optimization
  • sequence → subtype → image → outcome
